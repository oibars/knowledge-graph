## Thesis
AI doesn't kill Agile by replacing developers; it exposes Agile as scaffolding around human slowness that no longer fits a world of instant execution.

## Key claims
- Agile is a workaround for human limitations, not a philosophy.
- Scrum adapted to organizational friction rather than removing it.
- The bottleneck has moved from execution to decisions and governance.
- When execution is abundant, process structure becomes latency.
- Change Approval Boards are the archetypal form of organizational inertia.

## Quotes

## Reactions
- Agree that execution is no longer the constraint, but Neal underestimates how much of "Agile" is actually political packaging that makes governance tolerable — remove the ceremony and the governance doesn't disappear, it just becomes visible.
- The Team Topologies frame fits here: cognitive load is now on decision surface area, not build surface area.
- Open question: does this imply fewer engineers, or the same engineers doing different (decision-heavy) work?
